The Honest Watering Guide For Indoor Plants

Nearly every plant that comes back to our nursery for a health check has the same problem, and it is never neglect. It is love, delivered weekly, in 200ml doses, whether the plant asked for it or not.

Stop watering on a schedule

A calendar knows nothing about your radiator, your window direction or how humid last week was. Soil does. Push a finger two centimetres into the pot: if it comes out damp, wait. If it comes out dry and clean, water thoroughly until it runs from the drainage hole.

Water deeply and less often. Shallow sips keep the top wet and the roots thirsty.

The five signs you are overwatering

  • Yellowing lower leaves that feel soft rather than crisp
  • Soil that smells sour or looks green on the surface
  • Fungus gnats hovering when you move the pot
  • Stems that darken and soften near the soil line
  • New growth that emerges small and immediately browns

Getting drainage right

No plant survives standing water. Every Planto pot ships with a drainage hole and a matching saucer. If you use a decorative outer pot, lift the plant out to water it, let it drain for ten minutes, then put it back.

Seasonal adjustments

From November to February most indoor plants roughly halve their water use. Keep the same method — check the soil — and you will naturally water less without thinking about it.

If you take one thing from this article: the finger test beats every watering app ever written.
